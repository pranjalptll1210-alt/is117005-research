## How the research was assembled

1. Use primary sources for numerical claims and technical practices.
2. Read current BLS occupational profiles and keep one projection period across the comparison.
3. Distinguish a forecast for an occupation from an adjacent AI specialty.
4. Use WEF for global employer sentiment without combining its figures with U.S. forecasts.
5. Synthesize technical responsibilities into readable career profiles; label suggested entry paths and projects as recommendations.
6. Link evidence near the claim and keep this register for review.

## Evidence categories

**Official occupation:** a named BLS category with a projection. **Adjacent occupation:** a broader category used only as context for an AI specialization. **Global role family:** an employer-survey signal such as AI and ML specialists. **Emerging specialty:** a technically plausible career direction for which this project has not established a separate growth rate.

## What this project does not measure

The original overview does not rank every AI occupation or forecast salaries. The separate NY/NJ directory now examines a purposive sample of employer postings, with its verification limits documented. Emerging titles are illustrative and may overlap. The research is a dated snapshot; revisit sources before using it for a personal career decision.

## Updating the research

When revising a number, update the projection period, home-page cards, research table, relevant career profile, and this register together. Do not mix archived 2024–2034 figures with the current 2025–2035 series. Record new primary sources and distinguish evidence from interpretation.
